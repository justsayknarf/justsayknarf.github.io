_Right now, we're in the Geocities/Angelfire era of AI interaction_

There's a lot of AI generated content out there, but I feel that a lot of the conversation is missing the genuine feeling underneath it: this feeling of being a teenager again, actually surfing the web, finding content, copy and pasting code snippets / skills, and showcasing their Geocities pages with a sense of pride and wonder.

This is COOL. It's fun, it's playful, it's harmless (environmental impact aside). The world needs more whimsy. (Maybe fewer under construction gifs and autoplaying midi files, but you get it.)

But why is it creeping so hard into actual paid products?

Looking back, there's no way any of those sites should be championed as industry leading. When you think of a Geocities or Angelfire page today, you don't think "oh wow, innovation is _everywhere_."

The sheer volume of output, these sparkly, saccharine Geocities pages that are being force fed into professional products are empty skins that seem to deflate upon inspection.

Why is the bar lowered now that ideas can be generated faster?

Sure, there are people that will pay money for these products, but if I'm being honest, it feels exploitative — this preys on people who can't tell the difference between craft and generated gloss, whether that's a whole app or a feature slipped into something they already trust.

There's always been a tension between product definition and the builders, wishing that everything could be built faster. But now that writing code is replaced with code generation, things ARE being built faster. And the bottleneck has moved up the product development lifecycle, to the actual piece of product development that is the easiest to skip now that everything comes out with shiny coats of paint: learning and refining.

Slop only becomes slop when experiments are shipped as final products.

Experimentation and discovery are necessary for craft. But we all have to remember, especially since experiments are higher fidelity than ever, that the experiment isn't the shippable product.

Look, perhaps there's a bit of doomerism in me. I know cynicism and exploitation win more often than not — I'm choosing to care about craft anyway.
