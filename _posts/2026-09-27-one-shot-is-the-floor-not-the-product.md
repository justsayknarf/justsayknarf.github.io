title: The one-shot is the floor, not the product
description: Celebrating the wand isn't the same as practicing the craft.
tags: ai
---

One-shotting IS getting more impressive by the day. Reddit is full of people showing off what they've built with a single prompt — full Minecraft clones, RuneScape clones, Mario Kart clones, and now a full World of Warcraft clone.

Honestly, it's mindblowing.

My opinion hasn't changed: "0-to-1" work is becoming synonymous with "one-shot." But I'm absolutely floored by how fast things are evolving. The speed to concept is dizzying — we've come further than most of us expected, this fast. The impact on the indie game scene is going to be massive, and I can't wait to see it.

One-shotting has become the new benchmark, and everyone wants to set the next one with their magic wand. More people are celebrating what the wand produced than thinking about what it means to practice the craft itself.

The one-shot is the floor, not the product. These posts celebrate speed to demo.

I care about what actually gets put into the world. Why does it exist? Who's it for? Is it something to build on, or something to throw away?

Maybe this is just one more voice complaining about slop, and maybe I'm not giving enough credit to the people posting "look what I one-shotted." But watching games that teams lovingly designed and iterated on for years get reduced to "lol one-shot cloned" isn't something I can shrug off. The hard part was never the demo. For any product that requires craft, it's always been the last twenty percent.
