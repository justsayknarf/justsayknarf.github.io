One-shot prompts ARE impressive. When you see someone [one-shot a Minecraft clone](https://x.com/iam_elias1/status/2064968959888437350), it's tempting to assume your own roadmap should compress the same way. But remember, these are demonstrations of capability, not demonstrations of product development.

It's genuinely wild watching models push math and science into territory nobody thought possible — and that's a fair comparison to make elsewhere. But research and software aren't the same game. Software is built for someone else to use, so there's always some distance between builder and consumer, unless the two are the same person. Research doesn't carry that gap the same way.

So let's review what's actually happening and slow down just a bit.

My point from [my earlier post](/2026/07/31/the-red-herring-of-one-shotting-capabilities/) is that the one-shot capabilities absolutely accelerate product development, and it's easy to jump to conclusions: SaaS is dead, anyone can build any feature with a prompt, etc. The time between idea and feature is rapidly decreasing, yes. But it's important to examine which parts of the product process are changing.

Idea -> Prototype can be completely owned by any product thinker. Prototype and MVP are already interchangeable.

But MVP does NOT mean production ready, so let's pause mid-swing as you take an axe to all your product processes.

The next question to ask as a product leader: what are the requirements between MVP and MLP (minimum lovable product)? Who needs to be happy? What metrics do you need to see? If you release 20 new MVPs at once (obviously an exaggeration), what signals are you actually looking for? Shipping volume isn't validation.

Getting to the MVP is faster than ever. But remember, the MVP's purpose is to validate/invalidate an idea, and only the customer can do that validating.

Save the self-validation for your daily affirmations; let's keep it out of the roadmaps.
