There's an obsession in the influencer world with "one-shotting" huge prototypes as proof of a model's capability.

But one-shotting is a demo of capability, not fit-for-purpose. The value of an agent isn't the output — it's staying in the loop on the decisions that produced it.

The ability to one-shot an entire complex project from a single sentence is real utility, but it's step one of iterating, not the finish line. What the agent hands back sits somewhere on a spectrum of accuracy to what you actually wanted. We want that agent to read our minds and fill in gaps we didn't know we had, the scaffolding required for future decisions.

So herein lies the issue: for a real project, every assumption will be revisited eventually. A prototype is a direct result of input plus this variance. Models will keep improving, and that variance will keep shrinking and the scaffolding will improve. But remember, it will never disappear. There is no such thing as a one-size fits all.

This has always been true of commissioned work, long before AI. Commission a piece from an architect, and you're relying on their expertise to fit your wants into what's structurally possible. When money and time are exchanged, you're building a contract for what the work will be — and the mark of a professional is transparency about the decisions along the way, so the client can trust the outcome and still steer it.

This is the same trade that builders make with agents too. It matters more now than ever: people are increasingly allergic to AI-generated slop, which raises the bar an agent has to clear. Left unguided, a prediction engine's ceiling is mediocrity — the average of everything it's seen. Guide it, and the ceiling moves, but the mechanism doesn't: it's still an average, just of the scope you implied in the prompt. The strength of what comes back is always a function of how much scope you give it.

So what does this mean?

Know your expertise, communicate it to the agent. Gate progress on those decisions.

For parts you don't understand, design your system so that you have a paper trail for a future expert, or even a future agent.

Clearing the bar of mediocrity means designing ways to stay in the loop. Not "one-shotting" your prompt and hoping for the best.
